## Why your LinkedIn and X images didn't match

Every platform crops the feed image to a different shape. A 1080×1350 (4:5) Instagram image gets its top and bottom cut on LinkedIn and X. So every post here exists in three native shapes, built from the same layout:

| File suffix | Size | Ratio | Use on | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `-ig` | **1080 × 1350** | 4:5 | Instagram feed, Facebook feed, Threads | Tallest shape Instagram allows, takes the most screen in the feed |
| `-li` | **1200 × 1200** | 1:1 | LinkedIn (company page and personal) | LinkedIn shows 1:1 uncropped on desktop and mobile. 4:5 also works but gets cropped in some previews |
| `-x` | **1600 × 900** | 16:9 | X (Twitter) | X shows 16:9 in full in the timeline. Portrait images are cropped to 16:9 in the feed |

Other sizes already in the file (pages 01–07): Instagram Story / Reels cover 1080×1920, LinkedIn banner 1584×396, X header 1500×500, Facebook cover 1640×624, link/OG image 1200×630, YouTube banner 2560×1440 and thumbnail 1280×720, avatars 1000×1000.

Export from Figma: select the frame, Export, PNG, 2x for feed posts (platforms downscale, 2x keeps type crisp). Keep files under 8 MB for X.

## Editing in Figma

Each post is one auto-layout frame named `W1-01 / IG·FB·Threads 1080x1350`, `W1-01 / LinkedIn 1200x1200`, `W1-01 / X 1600x900`. To change copy, edit the Headline and Subhead text layers in all three. Orange emphasis is a character-range fill on the headline; re-apply it after retyping. The "Tag" in the top-right (WEEK 1 · 01) and the footer label (LAUNCHING 16 NOV) are plain text layers.
